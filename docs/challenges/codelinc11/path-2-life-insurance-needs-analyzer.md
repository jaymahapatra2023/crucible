# codeLinc 11 — Path 2: Life Insurance Needs Analyzer

**Build a conversational AI tool to right-size life insurance coverage.**

codeLinc 11 is Lincoln Financial's 24-hour student hackathon, 3–4 October 2026, Greensboro, NC.
Teams of three to eight choose one of two paths. This is Path 2.

## 1. The problem

Life insurance provides financial support to the people who depend on someone financially if
that person dies. Most people do not know how much coverage they need, find the products
confusing, and are put off by the maths. A tool that walks a person through their own situation
and explains a recommendation calmly and clearly helps them take charge of a decision they
otherwise avoid.

## 2. Core requirements

The tool must:

1. **Walk a user through their situation in conversation** — dependents, income, debts and
   existing coverage. The inputs that shape a person's need are: the people who depend on their
   income; the income that would need to be replaced; mortgage and other debts; education or
   future family expenses; existing employer or personal coverage; and the amount they can
   comfortably afford.
2. **Produce a personalised needs assessment with a clear explanation of the reasoning behind
   it.** The user sees a coverage amount (or range) and understands which of their answers drove
   it and how.
3. **Communicate the math clearly, without triggering anxiety or confusion for the user.** The
   calculation is visible and explained in plain language; the tone is supportive; the user is
   never alarmed into a number they cannot question.

## 3. Stretch goals

1. **Explain the difference between term and whole (permanent) life insurance.** Term insurance
   is coverage for a defined period, generally designed to provide straightforward protection and
   often used for time-bound needs. Permanent insurance is designed to remain in place longer and
   may include features beyond the death benefit, depending on the product.
2. **Surface the specific trade-offs that apply to the user's own scenario** — for example a
   young family with a mortgage and a fixed horizon versus a person seeking lifelong coverage with
   additional features — rather than a generic comparison.

## 5. What a strong entry demonstrates

- **A real conversation.** The tool asks for what it needs, in an order that makes sense, adapts
  to the answers, and does not demand a form's worth of fields up front.
- **Reasoning the user can check.** The needs assessment shows its inputs and its arithmetic; a
  user can change an answer and see the effect.
- **Calm, honest communication.** No fear-based framing; uncertainty is stated; the tool does not
  present itself as personalised financial advice or as a quote.
- **Grounded product explanations.** Term versus permanent is explained correctly and in terms of
  the user's own situation, without inventing product features, prices or guarantees.
- **Care with personal data.** Sensitive answers (health, income, dependents) are handled with
  restraint: not logged needlessly, not sent where they need not go.
- **Runs.** The application builds and starts from its repository as documented, so a judge can
  use it.

## 6. Deliverables and judging

Teams present on Sunday morning, 4 October, 10:00–12:00. Each team submits its repository. The
entry is judged on what the code does, the demo, the README, and the design documentation the team
kept while building (a spec is the team's best answer to "how did you build this?"). Prizes are
awarded at 12:45.
